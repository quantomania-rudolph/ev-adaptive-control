What happens when the powertrain can't deliver what the controller asks?

An adaptive controller learns one motor number while the car drives. When the battery or inverter suddenly can't supply the voltage it asked for, a plain controller blames its own number, keeps changing it, and runs away. This page replays that failure next to the protected controllers, and shows every channel the powertrain exposes along the way.

Every stage, with its live numbers

3 · Where the voltage goes missing

Asked for versus delivered

The upper chart is the run chosen in section 1. The controller asks for a voltage (dashed). The limiter delivers at most the ceiling (thin line). The gap between asked and delivered is the actuator disturbance from the research notes. The lower chart compares that gap across runs.

4 · Does the state stay bounded?

Protected runs stay inside the envelope, plain ones leave it

V is the energy-like Lyapunov candidate: tracking error plus estimate error. A bounded V means a bounded state. On a log scale the plain controller's V climbs by orders of magnitude, while the protected runs rise briefly and fall back. This is evidence from simulated runs. It is not a proof.

5 · Battery and DC link

A battery fault becomes a voltage fault

When the BMS cuts the pack current below what the inverter wants, the capacitor covers the gap for a few milliseconds, then the bus voltage falls, and with it the inverter ceiling.

6 · Electrical mismatch

Shaft power is not the whole electrical story

The theory work is on electrical quantities, so the electrical gap has to be rebuilt from the shaft gap, not swapped for it. Electrical power splits into shaft power, copper heat and stored magnetic energy. The gap splits the same way, and the three parts add up to the electrical gap. The run is the one chosen in section 1.

10 · Solid and not yet solid

What this page does and does not show

Checked in the sandbox

  • 60 automated tests pass: plant physics, telemetry identities, battery energy balance, and 51 data-loader cases.
  • No hidden clipping: the plant-versus-ours voltage check (M3) stays at about 1e-11 V in every run.
  • Every run is finite and none diverged. The real-drive runs use a Tesla Model 3 trace from TUM (CC BY 4.0).
  • The battery energy chain closes to about 1e-11 W at every step.

Not yet true

  • No stability proof. The inequality V̇ ≤ −λV + c is the theory team's job.
  • Pack numbers are generic assumptions. The simulated battery power tracks the real car only loosely (correlation 0.65 at 5 s smoothing, regen share 12% against 38%).
  • The real-drive vehicle is a scaled toy. The two highway tracks are refused by the Tier 1 motor.
  • No sensor noise yet. One unknown parameter, one kind of event per run.
  • Charts are thinned for the page. Full-rate data is in the run files.
Data: Gym-Electric-Motor 3.0.3 PMSM plant, our battery and DC-link model, and a Tesla Model 3 CAN trace (Rosenberger et al., TUM 2024, doi:10.14459/2024mp1735471, CC BY 4.0). Built from dashboard/build_dashboard.py.
