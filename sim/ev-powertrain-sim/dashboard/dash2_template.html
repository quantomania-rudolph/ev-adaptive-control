Powertrain Mismatch Board

A toy electric-vehicle motor drive. The controller asks for a voltage, the inverter delivers a different one, and the shaft gets a different torque than requested. This page shows how big each of those gaps is, and whether the data behind them can be trusted.

3. Can the data be trusted?

Automatic checks on every stored run file: time moves forward, no missing or infinite values, every column documented, and energy books closing.

Known limits
  • Energy gaps (shaft and electrical) survive any logging rate. Checks built on derivatives, such as the magnetic power term, need the full 0.1 ms logs and are marked invalid on thinned files.
  • Stored seed-0 rows are sampled every 17 steps so they do not line up with the 10-step speed loop. Averages are unbiased; individual spikes can still fall between rows.
  • Battery curves, sensor noise, inverter dead-time and BMS timing are assumed values, not measurements of a real pack.
  • The Tesla trace needs up to 1.5 times the small motor's torque, so it is scaled and some steps are infeasible by design.
